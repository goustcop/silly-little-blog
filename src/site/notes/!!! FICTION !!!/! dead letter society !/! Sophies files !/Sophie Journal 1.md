Today i was given a set of dice. It was small gift from a child i met while on a job. Thats all he was, a child met on the job. 

I had been tasked by the society to deal with a loose end. The loose end in question being a vampire who hadnt payed their dues and had been leaching of the societys resorces for decades. Plus he created a half breed. It was disgusting. 

The target was a man named Arthur. I want sent to kill the halfbreed, as a debt payed to the society. Finding my way to his quarters was a challenge. He was aware the society knew what he had done and tried to stay secretive. Which did not work. The house was a shack, no place for a vampire to live. Wood paneling coming loose, leaks from the ceiling, etc. It was a wreck. For a second, i wondered why raise a child in such a place, but i put that out of my mind. I could not afford empathy, i can never afford it. The wife was there, i had to kill her. I made it quick and painless. None of this was her fault. I hid the body and waited patiently for the child to come home. It had taken an hour or two, i brought a book to pass the time. Suddenly the door swung open, and there stood the boy. Schooled aged, bright eyed, and still in uniform with his bag of books hanging off his right shoulder. 

"hello little one. How was school?" I said, not sure why. I could have killed him where he stood, but for some reason, his bright eyes captivated me. I felt sick, i felt rotten. Looking in his eyes, empathy came back. And i wasnt sure what i could do. 

"Who are you?" the boy said, taking a step back. 

"Im no one you need to worry about. I only came to visit your mother, she left for... some reason. i didnt catch why, but it seemed important. She told me to look after you for the time being." I said.

"Ok! Well whats your name?" He asked, putting down his bag on the rickedy table that stayed near the door. 

And so it begun. We sat down to play a game as i stalled. As we played, my thoughts began to ruminate. It had been years since this has happend. Sometimes i hesitated, and those were signs of weakness. They tend to bubble up at the worst of times. 

He asked me if i had played with dice before. I looked at him puzzled before responding, "Well yes, but i dont have a set of my own". 

"hold on" the little boy said,before running off to a room in the back. I quickly stood to head after him, but before i could turn the corner, there he was with a small bag in his hand. He reached up and said "here, for you! so you can play games too!"

I still have that set of dice, because they are pretty. They were made of shiny stone and written on with paint. It had a rustic charm to it. 

I wont miss that kid.
